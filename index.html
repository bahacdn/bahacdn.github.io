Ideal response writing

A user writes in Sudanese dialect. I write the answer a good assistant should give, used as training data for chatbots.

User message (Sudanese)

عندي معاينة شغل بكرة وخايف شديد، أعمل شنو؟

Ideal response

من الطبيعي أن تشعر بالتوتر قبل المقابلة، وهذا يعني أنك تهتم بالفرصة. إليك ثلاث خطوات بسيطة لهذه الليلة:

١. راجع وصف الوظيفة، واكتب ثلاثة أمثلة من خبرتك تناسب متطلباتها.
٢. تدرّب بصوت عالٍ على الإجابة عن سؤال «حدّثنا عن نفسك» في دقيقة واحدة.
٣. جهّز ملابسك وأوراقك من الليلة، ونم مبكراً.

وقبل الدخول، خذ نفساً عميقاً ببطء. بالتوفيق!

Writing choices
  • Understood the Sudanese terms: معاينة means a job interview, شديد means “very”.
  • Replied in simple, warm MSA so the answer reads well to any Arabic speaker, as most style guides require.
  • Acknowledged the feeling first, then gave short, concrete steps the user can do tonight.

Annotation guideline excerpt

A page I wrote for a dialect-labeling task. Clear rules are what keep many annotators consistent.

Dialect labeling · Rules 1–6
  1. Label by the strongest word-level marker (for example هسع, دلوقتي, هلق), not by spelling or punctuation.
  2. If markers point to two or more dialects, label AMBIGUOUS and list the candidates in the notes.
  3. English words inside an Arabic sentence do not change the dialect label.
  4. Religious and formulaic phrases (إن شاء الله، والله، الحمد لله) are used everywhere and are not dialect markers.
  5. Sentences of fewer than four words with no unique marker are labeled AMBIGUOUS.
  6. Never guess from the topic or a place name. A Cairo street name does not make a sentence Egyptian.

Did picking big tech stocks beat a simple index fund?

Monthly prices for seven well-known companies against the S&P 500 and NASDAQ, September 2004 to June 2022.

  1. Ask

    Business task: A long-term investor wants to know whether buying individual large tech stocks rewarded the extra risk compared with holding an S&P 500 index fund.

    Stakeholder: an individual investor or a financial advisor building a long-term portfolio.

  2. Prepare

    Data: monthly closing prices from Yahoo Finance for IBM, Apple, Microsoft, Xerox, Amazon, Alphabet (Google), Adobe, Dell, the S&P 500 and the NASDAQ Composite, January 1990 to June 2022. The file is the public sample shipped with the Matplotlib library.

    Credibility check: I matched the last prices against published closes (Apple $137.44 and Microsoft $256.48 on June 28, 2022).

  3. Process
    • Removed 133 completely empty rows and 1 duplicate end-of-period row: 524 raw rows became 390 clean months.
    • Companies listed at different times (Amazon 1997, Google 2004, Dell 2016). To compare fairly, I used the common window where all tickers have data: Sep 2004 to Jun 2022, 214 months. Dell was dropped because its data only starts in 2016.
    • Verified zero missing values in the final window.
    df = pd.read_csv("stocks.csv", comment="#", parse_dates=["Date"])
df = df.dropna(how="all", subset=df.columns[1:])        # empty rows
df = df[~df.duplicated(subset=df.columns[1:])]          # duplicate row
window = df.set_index("Date").loc["2004-09":, tickers]  # common period
assert window.isna().sum().sum() == 0
  4. Analyze

    For each asset I calculated annual growth rate (CAGR), volatility (annualized standard deviation of monthly returns), and the worst peak-to-bottom drop.

  5. Share
    • Every single stock was riskier than the index. Volatility ranged from 21% to 36%, against 15% for the S&P 500.
    • Higher risk did not guarantee higher return. Xerox was as volatile as Apple and lost money. IBM grew slower than the index.
    • The winners only look obvious in hindsight. These companies were chosen because they are famous today. Investors in 2004 could not know which ones would become Apple and which would become Xerox.
    • Patience mattered. The S&P 500 peaked in October 2007, bottomed in February 2009, and only regained its peak in March 2013.
  6. Act
    • Make a broad index fund the core of a long-term portfolio. It gave solid growth with the lowest risk.
    • Keep any single company to a small share of the portfolio, and only with money that can stay invested through a 50%+ drop.
    • Plan for a recovery period of five years or more after a major crash.
    • Next step: repeat with dividends included and with a wider list of companies, including ones that were delisted, to remove survivorship bias.

When should an airline add capacity and staff?

Twelve years of monthly passenger demand for international airlines, 1949 to 1960.

  1. Ask

    Business task: Find the demand pattern so operations can schedule extra flights, staff and maintenance at the right time of year, and estimate demand for the next year.

    Stakeholders: network planning, crew scheduling and maintenance managers.

  2. Prepare

    Data: the “AirPassengers” dataset: monthly totals of international airline passengers in thousands, January 1949 to December 1960, 144 months. Originally published by Box & Jenkins and distributed with the R statistics language.

    Credibility check: my total of 40,363 thousand matches the published total for the dataset.

  3. Process
    • Reshaped the year-by-month table (12 × 12) into one row per month for time-series analysis.
    • Checked for gaps and invalid values: 144 of 144 months present, all positive whole numbers.
    SELECT year,
       SUM(passengers)                       AS annual_total,
       ROUND(100.0 * MAX(passengers) / MIN(passengers) - 100) AS peak_vs_low_pct
FROM   air_passengers
GROUP  BY year
ORDER  BY year;

    • The gap between the busiest and quietest month grew from 44,000 to 232,000 passengers, but in percentage terms it stayed between 37% and 63%. Seasonality grows with the business, so it should be planned in percentages, not fixed numbers.
    • Growth slowed in 1954 (6.2%) and 1958 (3.4%), years of US economic recession, so the forecast needs a margin for downturns.

  6. Act
    • Add capacity June to September, with the most extra flights and crew in July and August (about 24% above an average month).
    • Schedule heavy maintenance, training and staff vacation in November and February, when demand is 15–17% below average.
    • Plan 1961 at about 6.2 million passengers (9% growth, the 3-year average), with July and August near 640,000 each. Keep a lower case ready in case of a recession.
